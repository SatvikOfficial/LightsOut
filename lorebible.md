LIGHTS OUT — The Lamplit Chronicle
The lore bible: cosmology, ages, factions, classes, bosses, rules of the Street, and the truth beneath it all.
0. How to use this bible
Pillars.
• Deep time (the Tolkien layer). The Street has ages, songs, a founding myth, its own tongue, and ruins that predate the people who wander them. Everything has a name and a reason to be there.
• Every side is right and guilty (the Martin layer). The lamplords were tyrants who also kept the world safe. The match-makers freed the poor and broke the world. Nobody in this setting is simply good.
• The cosmos counts and does not care (the Lovecraft layer). The great power here is not evil. It is an accountant. The horror is not malice but indifference and completion. Knowledge costs.
Tone. Elegiac, patient, dry, a little tired. Short sentences. Never explain the Counter.
Reveal in layers. Street rumor (loading tips, bark lines) → class lore (lobby) → Memories (found in play) → the Codex (unlockable) → the Final Truth (end-game). Players who only skim should still get a coherent mood.
Originality note. Names, places and factions here are original. Influences are structural (deep history, moral grey, cosmic indifference), not borrowed names. Do not add names from existing franchises.
1. Cosmology
The Unlit
Before anything, there was not darkness but the Unlit: the condition of not-yet-seen. The Unlit is not evil and not hungry. It has no regard for anything. Walkers say dark when they mean the shadow of a tree. They say unlit when they mean the other thing. Whisper’s Memory says it best: the other side is not dark, only unlit.
The Choir of Nine Wicks
The world exists because nine voices called the Wicks sang a flame into the Unlit to make room. This is the Lampsong. The Wicks were not gods. They were something like the first idea of a lamp. Each sang one verse: Kindling, Warmth, Direction, Memory, Welcome, Warning, Mercy, Patience, and the ninth.
The Nine Candle Rites, practised by scholars like Mordai, are the surviving fragments of the eight verses anyone remembers.
The Ninth Wick
The ninth Wick sang nothing. It did not refuse. It simply fell silent at the end of the song, and that held note became the Hollow Sea, the shoreline of the Unlit. The howl that came up out of the sea, long before any hound, was the Ninth Wick’s unsung verse. Vargulf was bred from it.
The Counter (Ondukesh)
Beneath the third cage, something counts. In Wickish it is Ondukesh, the Debt-Counter. It is the thing the Unlit left behind when the Lampsong made the world a place that could be reckoned.
The Counter does not hate light. It counts it, and counting is all it is. A counted thing is entered in the Ledger. An entered thing leaves the world a little. Do this long enough and the world is a finished sum.
Rule for writers: the Counter never speaks, never threatens, never bargains. It is felt as an absence, a tick, a number going up.
2. The Ages of the Street
Dates use S (the Striking) as year zero: Before the Striking (BS) and After the Striking (AS).
The Age of Song (before years). The Lampsong sung, the Street laid down as a single road, the Long Lamp, running through the world.
The Long Lit (c. 900 to 1 BS). The Lamp Houses grow around six great lamps. Keepers tend them. The First Keeper makes the Tally Pact.
The Tally Pact (c. 700 BS). The First Keeper, tending the Origin Lamp at the Crossing, bargains with the Counter: the Street may stay lit if every lamp is counted. The Counter may count; the Street keeps its light. In the Pact’s final act the First Keeper is carried into his own light, one candle at a time. His body becomes the Origin Lamp’s wick.
The Quarrel (c. 150 to 1 BS). Lamplight is rationed. The Houses charge lamp-tax. The poor live in dimness. The lampless hamlets grow resentful.
The Snuffed Supper (3 BS). At the Feast of Nine Candles in the city of Ostelun, the Lamplords invite match-maker delegates to sign a Concord. At the signing, every candle in the hall is snuffed and three hundred people die in the dark. It is never proved who gave the order. The Registry (see §4) was seated at the table.
The Striking (year 0). In the lampless hamlet, a hedge-smith named Hesper Quill invents the first match. That same night the Cinder Ward burns. The Count of the Cinder Ward, Dracula of House Drakul, dies in the fire but refuses to be counted. Both the Lamplords and the match-makers claim it was the other’s doing.
The Long Dim (0 to now). Personal flame replaces public lamps. The great lamps fail one by one. The city of Ostelun forgets the sun. Light becomes private, and so does danger.
The Vigil (now). Each dusk, walkers wake on the Street in masks, with a torch and a little fuel, to try to reach Noon: the Origin Lamp relit. None has. The Vigil restarts.
3. The Street and its Six Wards
The Street is both the Long Lamp road and the world around it. The Wards do not keep their places: each Vigil, the Counter’s reckoning lays them out anew, like ledger pages shuffled. This is why the map is different every session.
The Crossing is the world’s center and the only fixed place. The Origin Lamp stood here. The three cages sit around it.
Ashen Forest — the Ward of Pines
• House: Thornwick, warden-lords of the pine roads.
• Lamp: the Hollow Lantern, a lamp carved into a pine. It went out when the sap-amber ran dry.
• Today: grey pines, ash-fall, wolves that avoid light. Fenna’s ridge.
• Secret: the pines still weep amber, but it is old light, and it remembers.
Forgotten Graveyard — Gravemarch
• House: Harrowgate, keepers of the dead and the lamps over them.
• Lamp: the Mortuary Lamp, lit for the dead so they find their way. It went out and the dead stopped leaving.
• Today: crypts, crosses, iron fences, crooked pines. Bram’s ground.
• Secret: the dead are not restless, they are uncounted. Nobody entered their names.
Witchlight Mire — Marrowmere
• House: Vane, marsh-wardens and the best lamp-wrights.
• Lamp: the Marsh Lights, a string of small lamps on stilts.
• Today: will-o’-wisps drift where the lamps stood. They are the lamps’ ghosts.
• Secret: wisps follow anyone who carries a flame, and they report what they see.
Frostfall — the Pale Ward
• House: Skarn, hard, proud, and the last to fall.
• Lamp: the Frozen Lamp, the only great lamp still lit, sealed in ice. Nobody can reach it.
• Today: snow, breath-clouds, the aurora that is the Frozen Lamp’s glow bleeding upward.
• Secret: it burns because it is not counted. Skarn lamp-keepers hid it from the Ledger.
Cinder Wastes — Ember-hold
• House: Drakul, the dragon-sigiled, richest of the Houses.
• Lamp: the Cinder Lamp, fed by oil-stores that fed the great fire of the Striking.
• Today: ash, glass, cracked ground. The scar where the Count died.
• Secret: the first match was struck in the Hamlet, but the Cinder Ward burned because of lamp-oil, which was the Houses’ own doing.
The Forgotten Hamlet — Lowe
• House: none. It never had a lamp.
• Today: broken walls, cold hearths, silence.
• Secret: Lowe is forgotten by the Counter. Its people were never counted. That is why the match could be invented there, and why Vargulf was bred to find what is uncounted.
4. Factions
Every faction is right about something and wrong about something worse.
The Lamp Houses (the Lamplords). Light is order. The lamps kept the Unlit out for centuries, and the rationing, they say, was the price of survival. They are right that the lamps worked. They are wrong that the price was fair.
The Match-makers (the Matchwrights). Fire belongs to everyone. The Houses starved the poor of light and called it duty. They are right about the injustice. They are wrong about what the lamps were holding back.
The Long Wick. Monks who tend the Origin Lamp’s memory. They believe every stolen flame is a soul to be escorted. Lumen is a Sister of the Long Wick. They keep a kinder ledger than the Counter’s. It is still a ledger.
The Nine Candle Rites. Scholars of the Lampsong. They collect the eight surviving verses and try to reconstruct the ninth. Mordai keeps the Rites. They are right that the ninth verse matters. They do not know what it will cost to sing it.
The Ash Clans. Beyond the salt flats, the Ashfields people survived the Striking by being too poor and too far to count. Viktor is Clanfather. They respect strength because it was the only thing the Ledger could not enter.
The Registry (the Keshra). Clerks of the Counter. They believe being counted is salvation: to be entered is to be remembered. They sat at the Snuffed Supper. They bred Vargulf. They issue the masks. They are the only faction that understands the Counter, and the only one that has made peace with it. Both facts are the horror.
The Uncounted. The people of Lowe, and anyone else the Ledger never reached. Free in a way no one else is, and unprotected in a way no one else is.
5. The Rules of the Street (the lore behind the mechanics)
The torch. Light is how you see and how you are seen. Your flame is your eyes and your confession. The Street shows itself only to the lit, and the lit are visible to everything.
Fuel and drain. Flame burns the walker down. The brighter the flame, the heavier the tax. A lighthouse burns fast and is seen from far away.
Amber (Mael). Kept light. Resin from the pines that wept when the first lamps were lit, holding tiny things that once saw the Street at noon. Burning amber releases a stored sunrise. It is the only light that remembers day. In the Registry’s accounts it is a receipt, proof that something was paid. Bram’s amber is hush money for the dead, and the dead hold the receipt.
The Twelve-Second Grace. When your torch dies, the Counter waits twelve seconds before it enters you. This is the Pause, a courtesy it does not know it is extending.
Kills only land in the dark. A life is entered in the Ledger only when no light is witness. In firelight a blow is only a wound. In the dark it is accounting. This is the Law of Unlight, and it is why every walker fears the dark more than the blade.
Siphoning. The unlit lean on the lit and drink. It is not a crime in the Street. It is arithmetic.
Bait. A lure of fuel. It works because walkers are greedy and the Street knows it.
Focus. Burning one amber to refuel is the walker’s one act of reckoning on their own terms. It rings because a kept light is released.
The Ten Rungs (Ember Ranks). The names of each rung: 0 Wick · 1 Spark · 2 Kindling · 3 Cinder · 4 Ember · 5 Flame · 6 Brand · 7 Beacon · 8 Lighthouse · 9 Pyre · 10 Noon (FULL LIT). Noon is not power. It is the one rung where the Counter cannot enter you, because you are all light. Nobody has held it more than a moment.
Bloodlit. After three kills in a row, a walker’s flame turns the color the Counter likes. It burns slower and reads brighter to everything. The Street calls this being noticed.
The Mask. Each walker wears a Vow-mask. No one remembers receiving theirs. When a walker dies the mask cracks, and six seconds later the Street returns them, whole, minus one thing they will not notice losing. This is why the Vigil restarts and why no one remembers a previous one.
Echoes (the bots). The Street never holds a Vigil empty. The Counter fills missing flames with echoes: walkers from other Vigils, run again. They choose classes, seek amber, flee the dark and the Sealed, and some of them are better than the living because they have done this before.
Memories. The pale motes drifting over the Street are fragments of what the walkers forgot: pieces of the First Keeper, the Pact, the Quarrel. Standing in one returns a piece. It does not feel like learning. It feels like remembering.
The Sealed and their hoards. Slaying a Sealed bursts its hoard. This is the debt it was bound to repay, paid out. After a minute, the Counter re-lends it. The Sealed are strengthened in proportion to the Street’s brightness, because the Street asks of its debts what the Street is owed.
“The Street is full.” The Counter does not count past a certain number. A full Street is simply a finished page.
6. The Three Sealed (the cages)
Three cages stand 58 paces from the Crossing. They are Wards of Attention: they hold only while nobody looks at them too long. Linger, and the cage exhales. The Houses built them in the Long Dim to hold three things the Street could not kill.
Dracula, the Unburned — the first cage
Who. Count of the Cinder Ward, of House Drakul (Dracula means of the dragon’s line). He was in the Hamlet the night of the Striking to collect lamp-tax. The match was struck in his sight. What happened. He died when the Cinder Ward burned, but when the Counter reached to enter him, he refused: “I will not be a number.” He stands untouched in the ash. That is why he is the Unburned. Why he drains your torch. Unaccounted, he cannot be sustained by the Ledger. He feeds on light as a man drinks to forget. Why he hates light. Light is what killed him. Barks. “Put it out.” · “I was counted once. I declined.” · “How warm you are.” Phases (future). (1) Courtly, slow, drains. (2) Ash-storm: the arena fills with embers that fake torchlight. (3) Unburned: only touch in the dark kills, so he snuffs the room.
Vargulf, the Hollow Hound — the second cage
Who. Bred by the Registry’s kennel-masters from the Ninth Wick’s howl. A tool made to find uncounted flames, such as the people of Lowe. Why it hunts light. Its whole purpose is to find what the Counter missed. Your torch is the brightest uncounted thing in the world. What it is not. It is not cruel. It does not hate. It is loyal to a thing that never notices it. Barks. (growls only) · a howl that sounds almost like a verse. Phases (future). (1) Prowl. (2) The Crouch (telegraph) and the Loose (dash that kills through any flame). (3) Hunger: it begins to sing, drawing wisps toward whoever is brightest.
The Lamplighter — the third cage
Who. Once the lamplighter of Candle Row in Ostelun, the man who, on the night of the Snuffed Supper, walked the street snuffing each lamp on order. He never found out who gave the order. What it is now. A ghost carrying the lantern that was once his streetlight, collecting every light ever snuffed. His cage sits on the Counter’s seat. He is its hand. Why it is not full. The Ledger never stops growing. Each Vigil snuffs thousands. When it holds you, it remembers you. It carries each snuffed light’s last seen thing. It is the Street’s only gentle monster, and the one that wins. Barks. “Hold still. I will remember you.” · “Brighter is cruel. Dimmer is kind.” Phases (future). (1) Drift and Hold. (2) The Row: it walks the arena lighting a street of lamps and snuffing them in order. (3) The Counting: the Counter’s tick audible; every second you are lit adds to a number on screen.
Expansion Sealed (one per other ward)
• The Glasswarden (Frostfall). Warden of the Frozen Lamp. Immune while you are lit by anything but its own flame.
• Mother Marrow (Mire). Mother of the wisps. She spawns false walkers made of witchlight.
• The Unaccounted Smith (Cinder). Forged Korr’s chain. No registry knows him. Fights with a hammer that makes noise as light.
• The Hollow Lantern (Ashen Forest). A wooden lamp-spirit that grows a forest of dead lamps around it.
• The Uncounted King (Hamlet). Unarmed. Cannot be seen by echoes. The only boss the Counter cannot find, and the only one that finds you.
7. The Twelve Walkers
Each walker carries a sin, a ward they come from, and a secret the Counter has already written down.
Viktor, the Emberborn · Ash Clans · Wrath. Clanfather of the Ashfields. Struck by the last honest lightning the sky sent; it never left. His axe remembers every torch it has snuffed. Secret: he is not scarred by the lightning. He is fed by it, and it is rationed.
Fenna, the Ash-Eye · Ashen Forest · Envy. Counts lights from the ridge. Traded one eye to the thing in the well. Secret: the thing in the well is the Hollow Lantern’s root. It gave her eye back to the forest, and watches through it.
Mordai, the Candle-Sage · Nine Candle Rites · Curiosity. Keeps the Rites. His flame-darts are small prayers. Secret: he has found the ninth verse. He is afraid to finish it.
Whisper, the Veil-Witch · Marrowmere · Deceit. Her curse travels slow as debt and hits like collection. She keeps her veil drawn because the other side is not dark, only unlit. Secret: she has been to the Hollow Sea and came back. She is not entirely the one who left.
Bram, the Gravedigger · Gravemarch · Greed. Pays his meals in what he unearths. His amber is hush money so the dead keep quiet. Secret: he is paying off a debt to the Registry in installments, and it never goes down.
Lumen, the Lamp-Monk · The Long Wick · Gluttony. A Sister of the Long Wick. Believes every stolen flame is a soul she escorts. Her ledger says she keeps more than she takes. Secret: the ledger is wrong. She has not told the Order.
Korr, the Chain-Fist · Cinder Wastes · Grief. His chain is three fathoms and older than him. The smith who forged it is no longer accounted for in any registry. Secret: the smith is the Unaccounted Smith, and the chain is his apology.
Vessa, the Knife-Singer · wanderer · Vanity. Hums to keep time. When the humming stops, you have a second. The song is older than her, and her teachers are not. Secret: the song is one of the eight verses.
Torvold, the Lantern-Keeper · Ostelun · Pride. Last keeper of the streetlights after the city forgot the sun. His lamp swings wide and bright: mercy for friends, confession for enemies. The dark hates him personally. Secret: he was on Candle Row the night of the Snuffed Supper, holding a lamp.
Nyx, the Moth-Dancer · Frostfall · Longing. Drawn to every flame, loyal to none. Dances at the edge of your light in patterns almost like words. Nobody has read them; nobody who tried is around to argue. Secret: the patterns spell the Counter’s name in a script older than Wickish.
Grim, the Scythe-Warden · unplaced · Resignation. Not death itself, something more tired. Sent to hold the line while death was away. Secret: death has been away since the Striking. Nobody told Grim it is over.
Petra, the Ember-Drum · Hamlet of Lowe · Revelry. Her drum speaks in a language the dark half-understands. When it thuds after a kill, the whole street stumbles. Secret: Petra is uncounted. The drum is how Lowe tells the Street it still exists.

Ten more (ready to add)
1. The Wicks. Nine sang. Eight are remembered. The ninth is why the sea has a voice.
2. The Pact. The Keeper asked for nothing. That is why the Counter agreed.
3. The Supper. Three hundred candles went out in the same breath. The room did not get darker. It got emptier.
4. Hesper Quill. She did not mean to burn anything. She wanted to see her child’s face at night.
5. The Count Declines. He was offered a place in the Ledger. He said no. It is the only time anyone has.
6. The Salt Flats. The Ash Clans cross them because the Ledger does not reach that far. Nothing there is counted, so nothing there is kind.
7. The Mask. You were given it. You do not remember by whom. That is the first thing you forgot.
8. The Frozen Lamp. It is still lit. That is not good news. It is the one lamp the Counter has not been paid for.
9. The Ninth Verse. It is not a song. It is a silence that has been waiting to be finished.
10. Noon. Whoever reaches it will be allowed one breath of true daylight. Nobody has ever described the second breath.

• “Light your own.” (A Matchwright motto. A boast, a toast, a curse.)
• “Be counted.” (The Registry’s blessing. Also its warning.)
• “May your amber be old.” (A farewell.)
• “Don’t stand still on a street that’s gone out.” (A Lamplighter proverb.)

• “Noon is a rumor.” (Cynical; common among veterans.)
• “A flame with no witness is a rumor too.” (The Law of Unlight, in everyday speech.)
11. The Final Truth, and four endings
The truth. The Vigil is not a punishment. It is not a game, a trial, or a crusade. It is an inventory. The Counter counts lights because counting is the only thing it does. Each Vigil, it takes stock of every flame that can still be lit, and each death adds one to the Ledger. The Street is not being destroyed. It is being completed.
There are four things a walker who reaches Noon might do:
1. Kindle. Relight the Origin Lamp. The Lamp Houses rise again, with their rationing and their order, and the Tally Pact resumes. Safety, at the cost of everything the Matchwrights fought for.
2. Strike. Burn the Ledger. Fire is free again for everyone, and the boundary the Counter held against the Unlit is gone. Liberty, at the cost of the world’s edges.
3. Be Counted. Join the Registry. Peace, permanence, and the end of the self. The most comfortable ending, and the most frightening.
4. Go Unlit. Walk into the Hollow Sea. Understanding, and cessation. Whisper’s ending.
No ending is right. Every ending is argued for by someone the player has come to respect.
12. Canon locks
Anything below is already in the code and must not be contradicted:
• The match was struck once, on a night a count died without going out.
• The Three Sealed were old before the match; the Hound came from the sea’s howl; the Lamplighter’s lantern was once a streetlight.
• Something counts beneath the third cage.
• The First Keeper was carried into his own light, one candle at a time.
• Every walker has been dark, and the Street does not count how long you stayed.
• Viktor’s axe, Whisper’s veil, Bram’s hush money, and Petra’s drum are as written in their Memories.
• Torvold is the last keeper of the streetlights after the city forgot the sun.
• Lumen is a Sister of the Long Wick. Mordai keeps the Nine Candle Rites. Fenna traded an eye to the thing in the well. Korr’s smith is unaccounted for.
